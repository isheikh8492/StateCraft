# 0011. The dice decide how an order lands, and the game decides what is too vague

Date: 2026-11-12. Status: accepted.

## Context

Two things a model was trusted with turned out badly in play on a real local model
(`docs/research/sessions/2026-11-12-year-1.md` and `2026-11-12-landings.md`).

- **How an order went.** The judge chose among "as intended", "partial", "stalled" and
  "backfired". It chose "partial" for all twelve orders in a year and a half. An order that
  can only ever half-work has no suspense, and the choice was a fact about the world decided
  by a model, which the project's first rule forbids.
- **Whether an order was clear enough to act on.** The reader set aside "Open talks with
  tribal elders in Balochistan" and "Nationalise the three largest private banks" as too
  vague, asking how they should be implemented. Both were thrown away. An order lost that
  way is the worst thing that can happen to a player in a game of plain-language orders.

## Decision

- **Keyed dice decide the landing** before any model is asked, from fixed odds by how
  forceful the order is (placeholders): a small order goes as intended 45% of the time and
  goes wrong 15%; a large one 20% and 34%; an order outside the catalogue 25% and 30%.
  The same order in the same campaign always lands the same way.
- **The landing sets what the order may move.** As intended: the stronger half of each
  range the catalogue allows for that order. In part, or backfired: the weaker half.
  Stalled: nothing. An order that backfires also raises grievance, in its region or
  everywhere, by the limit for effects outside the catalogue.
- **The judge is told the landing** and asked for two things only: how far each variable
  moved inside the range it is given, and one sentence on why it went that way. The schema
  offers it no other result, and a reply that claims one is overruled.
- **A stalled or backfired order is news** on the day it was due to take effect, as an event
  of its own that cites the order.
- **The game's own reader has the last word on vagueness.** If the keyword rules can tell
  what an order means, or it is a full sentence of three words or more, it goes ahead: as the
  instrument the rules found, or as an order outside the catalogue. Only what the rules
  cannot read at all is answered in the world as too vague.

## Consequences

- The same seed gives a different campaign than before this change.
- A model's judgment of how an order would really go is no longer used. The odds know
  nothing yet about who opposes the order or how strong the government is; they should come
  to depend on standing, the legislature and the state of the country. Until then a popular,
  easy order stalls as often as a hated one of the same size.
- An order outside the catalogue that the model would rightly have questioned now goes
  ahead with small, bounded effects. That is the lesser harm: the player sees a result and
  can order again.
